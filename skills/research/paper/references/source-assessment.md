# Source assessment

Score every source before it enters a report. Record the verdict in the source notes so the reasoning survives into review.

## Credibility checks

|Dimension|Question|Red flag|
|---|---|---|
|Provenance|Who published it, and under what name?|Anonymous author, spoofed domain, no imprint|
|Evidence|Are claims backed by data, citations, or a method?|Only assertion, no traceable basis|
|Recency|Is it current for the topic?|Outdated statistics presented as current|
|Independence|Who funded or operates it?|Undisclosed sponsorship, single-party self-report|
|Integrity|Has it been corrected or retracted?|Retraction notice, heavy post-publication editing|
|Replicability|Can the result be checked elsewhere?|Exclusive data with no access path|

## Tiering

- **Primary**: original research, official statistics, court/government records, transcripts, first-party documents.
- **Secondary**: peer-reviewed reviews, established journalism citing primary records, meta-analyses.
- **Tertiary**: encyclopedias, aggregators, unattributed social posts — orientation only, never sole support for a claim.

## Rules

- Cite the strongest available tier; never use a tertiary source when a primary one exists.
- Conflicting sources: report the disagreement and its basis, do not average or pick silently.
- Numbers from any source are quoted with their date and scope attached.
- Sponsored or partisan sources may be cited with the affiliation disclosed, not silently laundered.
- When only weak sources exist, say so in the report instead of implying stronger support.
