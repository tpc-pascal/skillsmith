# Synthesis

Combine multiple sources into one answer. The unit of synthesis is a claim, not a source — organize by what is being asserted, then attach evidence to it.

## Evidence matrix

Build a small matrix before writing:

|Claim|Supporting sources|Conflicting sources|Confidence|
|---|---|---|---|
|The assertion being made|Tier-1/2 sources backing it|Sources disagreeing, with why|High / medium / low|

- One row per material claim; a claim with no row does not belong in the report.
- Confidence follows the weakest link: strong sources on one side and a real conflict on the other caps confidence at medium.

## Writing rules

- Merge overlapping findings; do not summarize source-by-source when a claim spans several.
- Keep provenance: every synthesized sentence must be traceable to matrix rows.
- Contradictions are surfaced with their basis (data, method, date), never averaged away.
- Distinguish what sources state, what they infer, and what you infer on top.
- Quotes are verbatim from one source; paraphrase combines and is marked as such.
- No new claims appear in synthesis that were not present in the sources.
